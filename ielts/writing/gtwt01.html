Part 1: Exam Overview & Assessment Breakdown

In IELTS General Training Writing Task 1, you are presented with a real-life scenario and asked to write a letter requesting information, complaining, apologizing, inviting, or explaining a situation:

  1. Time & Score Weightage:

    Spend no more than 20 minutes on Task 1, which accounts for 33% of your total IELTS Writing band score.

  2. Minimum Word Count Requirement:

    Write at least 150 words (aim for 165–190 words so every bullet point is developed thoroughly without wasting time).

  3. The Mandatory 3 Bullet Points Rule:

    Every prompt includes 3 bullet points. Dedicate one clear Body Paragraph (BP1, BP2, BP3) to each bullet point to guarantee full Task Achievement.

  4. Four Equal Marking Criteria (25% Each):

    Examiners grade your letter equally on Task Achievement (25%), Coherence & Cohesion (25%), Lexical Resource (25%), and Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%).

Part 2: The 3 Core Letter Styles, Salutations & Skeletons

Identifying the recipient immediately determines your register, greeting, paragraph flow, and closing sign-off:

  1. Formal Letter (High Authority / Stranger):

    Written to an unknown official, manager, municipal council, insurance company, or local newspaper editor using a strictly formal register.

    Salutation & Sign-Off: Dear Sir or Madam, → Opening Purpose → BP1 → BP2 → BP3 → Closing Line → Yours faithfully, + Full Name.
    Strategy: Never use contractions (such as don't, I'm, can't). End with a polite closing line like "I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience."
  2. Semi-Formal Letter (Known Acquaintance / Professional):

    Written to someone you know by surname or professional role, such as a landlord, colleague, teacher, local artist, playback singer, or radio jockey.

    Salutation & Sign-Off: Dear Mr. Sharma, (or Dear Ms. Verma, / Dear RJ,) → State the Purpose → BP1 → BP2 → BP3 → Closing Line → Yours sincerely, + Full Name.
    Strategy: Maintain polite warmth while keeping grammar structured and respectful.
  3. Informal Letter (Close Friend / Family Member):

    Written to a close friend, cousin, or sibling to invite them, give suggestions, apologize, or share personal news in a warm, conversational tone.

    Salutation & Sign-Off: Dear Ram, (First name only) → Warm Opening & Purpose → BP1 → BP2 → BP3 → Closing Line → Yours lovingly, (or Warm regards,) + First Name.
    Strategy: Natural contractions and expressive, friendly colloquialisms are encouraged here.

Part 3: Golden Classroom Rules & High-Scoring Connectors

Follow these strict classroom rules and linking structures to maximize Coherence & Cohesion:

  1. No Conclusion Paragraph in Task 1:

    Strict Rule: Never write a summary or conclusion paragraph in Task 1. Finish BP3 and write a single courteous closing line before your sign-off.

  2. Never Write the Word "BELOW":

    Avoid phrases like "listed below" or "in the details below" in writing tasks; refer directly to the information instead.

  3. Approximation & Time Range Precision:

    When exact numbers are unavailable, use qualifiers such as Approximately, About, Around, Nearby. Always pair time prepositions accurately: use From [X] to [Y] or Between [X] and [Y].

  4. Reporting Verbs (Data / Reference Context):

    Use Illustrates, Represents, Depicts, Demonstrates, Provides information about—never invent artificial synonyms for the word "graph".

  5. Essential Paragraph Connectors:

    Link your points smoothly across body paragraphs:

    Sequencing: First of all, Firstly, Initially
    Addition: Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition to this
    Result / Consequence: Hence, Therefore, Consequently, As a result
    Contrast: However, On the other hand, Nevertheless

Part 4: Solved Band-9 Model Letters & Practice Prompts

Study how each bullet point is expanded cleanly in these solved classroom templates:

  1. Model 1 — Formal Letter to Local Newspaper (Saving Public Park):

    Prompt: There is a public park near where you live. You have heard that the local council wants to sell this park. Write a letter to a local newspaper. In your letter: (1) introduce yourself, (2) describe the importance of the park, (3) say what action you will take if the council continues with its plan.

    Dear Sir or Madam,

    My name is Vikramjit Singh, and I am a longtime resident of 341 Ferozepur Road. I am writing to bring to your attention a deeply concerning proposal by the local municipal council to sell the public park situated in our neighborhood. This decision has caused widespread distress among local citizens, as it will lead to severe negative consequences for our community.

    Let me explain the situation in greater detail. There are approximately 3,000 residents in my locality who rely heavily on this green space. Around 400 to 500 individuals visit the park daily for jogging, morning walks, and outdoor exercise. Furthermore, this is the sole recreational area where senior citizens gather to socialize and children play safely. If the park is sold, residents will be forced to commute to the nearest alternative park located three miles away, which is impractical for elderly individuals on a daily basis.

    If the council proceeds with this plan, I will have no option but to organize a peaceful neighborhood petition and escalate the matter to higher authorities. I would be truly grateful if you could publish this issue in your esteemed newspaper to raise public awareness.

    Yours faithfully,
    Vikramjit Singh

  2. Model 2 — Informal Letter to a Friend (Landmark Birthday Celebration):

    Prompt: Your friend is about to celebrate a landmark birthday and would welcome suggestions as to how it should be celebrated. Write a letter to your friend. In your letter: (1) say what type of party should be held, (2) suggest a suitable venue, (3) make a recommendation as to entertainment.

    Dear Ram,

    I hope this letter finds you in high spirits! Since your milestone 30th birthday is right around the corner, I wanted to share a few exciting ideas to make sure we celebrate this unforgettable red-letter day in grand style.

    To make this celebration truly unique, I think you should host a vibrant theme-based costume party where all guests dress according to a retro Bollywood or Hollywood theme. A themed gathering instantly breaks the ice and creates a lively atmosphere for everyone attending.

    As for the venue, I strongly recommend booking the Orchid View Resort near your residence. It features spacious banquet lawns, exceptional cuisine, and a picturesque ambiance ideal for hosting a large group of friends and family. You should check their availability for next weekend at the earliest.

    Moreover, regarding entertainment, we can invite a talented local playback singer to perform live acoustic hits, followed by a DJ session and interactive party games for both youngsters and elders. I am eagerly looking forward to celebrating with you next week!

    Yours lovingly,
    Vikramjit

  3. Additional Practice Prompts (Formal & Semi-Formal):
    Formal Practice (Travel Insurance Claim): On a recent holiday you lost a valuable item. Fortunately, you have travel insurance to cover the cost of anything lost. Write a letter to the manager of your insurance company. In your letter: (A) describe the item you lost, (B) explain how you lost it, (C) tell the insurance company what you would like them to do.

    Semi-Formal Practice (Inviting a Local Artist): You are going to arrange a special party at your home next week. You want to invite a local artist who is a playback singer. Write a letter to him. In your letter: (1) explain the occasion, (2) invite him to the party, (3) tell him why you want him to perform.
