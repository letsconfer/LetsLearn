1. Task 2 Blueprint & Assessment Breakdown

IELTS Writing Task 2 requires you to write a formal discursive essay. It is worth 66% of your total writing score and demands at least 250 words completed within 40 minutes.

  • Time Limit: Spend strictly 40 minutes on Task 2 (after spending 20 minutes on Task 1).
  • Word Count: Write at least 250 words (Aim for 280–320 words for robust development).
  • Marking Criteria (25% each): Task Response (25%), Coherence & Cohesion (25%), Lexical Resource (25%), and Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%).
  • Golden Rule for Paragraphs: Follow the strict progression: Define → Rephrase your reasons in their body paragraphs → Explain → Example → Result/solution.

2. The 8 Types of IELTS Essays & Thesis Templates

Mastering Task 2 begins with accurately identifying the prompt type and anchoring it with the correct thesis statement formula in your introduction:

Type 1

Agree or Disagree

Prompt Focus: Do you agree or disagree?

Thesis Statement Formula:
I agree/disagree with this notion due to TS 1 and TS 2.

Type 2

Direct Question

Prompt Focus: Why does this happen? What measures can be taken?

Thesis Statement Formula:
This essay will attempt to analyze TS 1 and TS 2 and offers its conclusion.

Type 3

Problem / Solution

Prompt Focus: What are the problems and solutions?

Thesis Statement Formula:
This essay will articulate some of its problems such as TS 1 and TS 2 and propose some possible solutions to alleviate this.

Type 4

Advantage & Disadvantage

Prompt Focus: Discuss advantages and disadvantages.

Thesis Statement Formula:
This essay will explicate/articulate its merits and demerits in following paragraphs.

Type 5

Positive or Negative Development

Prompt Focus: Is this a positive or negative development?

Thesis Statement Formula:
I believe that this is positive/negative development due to TS 1 and TS 2.

Type 6

Advantage Outweigh Disadvantage

Prompt Focus: Do the advantages outweigh disadvantages?

Thesis Statement Formula:
I believe that its advantages are overlapping its disadvantages.

Type 7

To What Extent Agree or Disagree

Prompt Focus: To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Thesis Statement Formula:
I largely agree/disagree with the said notion due to TS 1 and TS 2.

Type 8

Both Views and Opinion

Prompt Focus: Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Thesis Statement Formula:
This essay will intend to analyze both the views along with my perspective.

3. Paragraph-by-Paragraph Essay Blueprint

A standard Band-9 essay follows a rigid 4-paragraph structure[cite: 30]:

Paragraph 1: Introduction (35–45 words)
1. Paraphrase the prompt background using synonyms.
2. Thesis statement / Outline sentence clearly stating your position.

Paragraph 2: Body Paragraph 1 (80–90 words)
1. Topic sentence introducing the first main idea.
2. Explanation and supporting evidence / example.

Paragraph 3: Body Paragraph 2 (80–90 words)
1. Topic sentence introducing the second main idea or opposing view.
2. Explanation and supporting evidence / example.

Paragraph 4: Conclusion (30–40 words)
1. Restate your main argument / opinion in different words.
2. Final concluding thought (no new ideas introduced).

Universal Paragraph Construction Formula

Every paragraph in your essay must follow a disciplined logical flow to secure maximum Coherence & Cohesion points:

Introduction Paragraph:
1. Rephrase the Topic: Paraphrase the prompt background using advanced synonyms.
2. Thesis Statement: Insert the exact thesis formula corresponding to your essay type.

Body Paragraphs (BP1 & BP2):
1. Define / Task Statement: Open with a strong transitional marker (e.g., To begin with) stating your core reason.
2. Explain: Clarify your argument in depth (e.g., In other words, / To elaborate it,).
3. Example: Provide a concrete real-world instance (e.g., For instance, / To exemplify,).
4. Result / Effect / Solution: Conclude the paragraph with an outcome or solution (e.g., As a result, / To tackle it,).

Conclusion Paragraph:
Summarize your main arguments and restate your final position clearly (e.g., In conclusion, / To conclude).

4. High-Scoring Start Phrase Toolkits

A. How to Start an Essay (Introductions)

  • It is true that...
  • Few of the people think that...
  • Most of the people think that...
  • It is often argued that... (Best for Both Views)
  • It is generally considered that...
  • No one can deny...
