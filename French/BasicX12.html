Guide with Detailed Orthography Logic and Pronunciation Breakdown

GROUP 1: -TION / -SION = ALWAYS LA

Rule: Nouns ending in -tion or -sion are consistently feminine (La).
Forms abstract/action nouns; pronounced with a nasal '-syon' / '-zyon' sound.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
- -tion and -sion suffixes indicate feminine gender in French nouns.
- The 't' in -tion is pronounced as an 's' sound followed by a nasal 'on', while -sion produces a '-zyon' sound.
- English Comparison: Suffixes '-tion' and '-sion' (e.g., nation, television).
- Hindi Comparison: '-स्योन' / '-जीयोन'।

GROUP 2: -MENT = ALWAYS LE

Rule: Nouns ending in -ment are consistently masculine (Le).
Forms masculine nouns/adverbs; pronounced with a nasal 'man' / 'mãh' sound where 't' is silent.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
- -ment is the standard French suffix equivalent to English noun/adverb formations.
- Following French suffix rules, the final 't' is silent, and the preceding 'en' creates a nasalized 'man' / 'mãh' sound.
- English Comparison: Adverb/noun suffixes with silent ending letters.
- Hindi Comparison: '-मान' / 'माँ'।

GROUP 3: -URE / -ENCE / -ANCE = 80% LA

Rule: Nouns ending in -ure, -ence, or -ance are mostly feminine (80% La).
Forms abstract and state nouns; pronounced with oral '-tyur' or nasal/oral '-ans' sounds.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
- -ure, -ence, and -ance endings predominantly denote feminine gender (approx. 80% rule).
- -ure is pronounced '-tyur' with a clear 'r', while -ence and -ance use '-ans' sounds.
- English Comparison: Cognates ending in '-ure', '-ence', and '-ance' (culture, difference, chance).
- Hindi Comparison: '-त्युर' / '-रान्स' / '-सान्स'।
