1. Ordinal Numbers (Les Nombres Ordinaux)

French: Les Nombres Ordinaux / Pronunciation: ले नॉँब्र ओर्दीनो

Definition: Ordinal numbers indicate position or order in a series (e.g., 1st, 2nd, 3rd / पहला, दूसरा, तीसरा).

How to Form Ordinal Numbers from Cardinal Numbers:
• General Rule (+ième): Add the suffix -ième to the cardinal number (e.g., deux → deuxième, trois → troisième).
• Words ending in 'e': Drop the final '-e' before adding '-ième' (e.g., quatre → quatrième, seize → seizième, trente → trentième).
• Spelling Adjustment for 5 (cinq): Insert a 'u' before adding '-ième' → cinquième.
• Spelling Adjustment for 9 (neuf): Change 'f' to 'v' before adding '-ième' → neuvième.
• Special Rule for 1st: 1st uses unique words with gender distinction: premier (masculine) and première (feminine). From 2nd onwards, ordinal numbers are gender-neutral.
• Compound Numbers ending in 1: 21st = vingt-et-unième, 31st = trente-et-unième, 41st = quarante-et-unième (do NOT use premier in compounds).

2. Dates in French (Les Dates)

French: Les Dates / Pronunciation: ले दात

The format of dates is very important for writing letters, making plans, or requesting appointments.

Standard Date Structure & Grammatical Rules:
• French always uses the formula: le + day number + month + year (e.g., le 18 avril 2013).
• The First Day Logic: When we talk about the first day of any month, the formula is slightly different — the **ordinal number** is used instead: le premier = 1er + month (+ year).
• For all other days (2, 3, 18, 31, etc.), use the regular **cardinal number**: le 2 mai, le 18 avril 2013.
Asking & Answering Questions about Dates:

• Question: Quelle est la date ?
  Pronunciation: केल ए ला दात ?
  Translation: What's the date? / आज क्या तारीख है?

• Question: Quelle est la date de ton anniversaire ?
  Pronunciation: केल ए ला दात द तों आनीवेर्सेर ?
  Translation: What is your birth date? / आपका जन्मदिन कब है?

• Answer Example (Standard Day): C'est le 18 avril 2013.
  Pronunciation: से ल दीज़-वीत आव्रील दु मील त्रेज़
  Translation: April 18, 2013 / 18 अप्रैल 2013

• Answer Example (1st Day of Month): C'est le premier mai.
  Pronunciation: से ल प्रमिये मे
  Translation: It is May 1st / यह 1 मई है

3. Telling Time (L'Heure / Dire l'heure)

French: Dire l'heure / Pronunciation: दीर लूर

It is important to tell time in French for traveling, meeting up with friends, making appointments, and getting to work or school on time.

Key Vocabulary & Notation Rules:
• heure = hour / time / o'clock (pronounced उर / ज़र with a guttural French R).
• Abbreviation 'h': The abbreviation h is used where a colon is used in English (e.g., 1h00 in French vs 1:00 in English, 7h51 in French vs 7:51 in English).
• Asking Time in French: Quelle heure est-il ? (What time is it?)
• Informal Expressions (used only with 1 to 12):
  - et quart = quarter past (:15)
  - et demie = half past (:30)
  - moins le quart = quarter to (:45)
  - moins [minutes] = minus minutes (e.g., moins dix = 10 to)

4. Mobile Numbers (Les Numéros de Téléphone)

French: Les Numéros de Téléphone / Pronunciation: ले नुमेरो द टेलीफ़ोन
How to Read & Write Phone Numbers in French:
• You should be very accurate with numbers, especially from 0 to 99.
• Phone numbers are shared and spoken in **groups of two digits** (pairs).
• Standard 10-digit format: XX XX XX XX XX.
Asking & Sharing Phone Numbers:

• Question: Quel est votre numéro de téléphone ?
  Pronunciation: केल ए वोत्र नुमेरो द टेलीफ़ोन ?
  Translation: What is your phone number? / आपका फोन नंबर क्या है?

• Question: Quel est ton numéro ?
  Pronunciation: केल ए तों नुमेरो ?
  Translation: What is your number? / तुम्हारा नंबर क्या है?
