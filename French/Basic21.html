Nouns Gender & Plural Rules

Noun Genders, Suffix Rules, and Pluralization Formulas

1. Gender of Nouns (Le Genre des Noms)

French: Le Genre des Noms / Pronunciation: ल जँर दे नॉँ

The biggest differences between French and English are the use of gender; each noun in French has a feminine or masculine gender. We can divide it into three categories of gender, masculine, feminine, and Neuter, but French has only two: masculine and feminine.

Please don't get confused about the concept of gender in French; it refers only to the noun as a word. Although in many instances when referring to a person the gender will correspond to that person's gender in general, in grammatical concerns it has nothing to do with the biological sex of the object or a person in the world. For example, un professeur can be either a male or female teacher, but the word itself is linguistically masculine.

It is very important to judge the gender of a noun so as to know how the word will be used and how other words are used along with it. There are no strict rules about determining the gender of nouns, but the majority of French words follow a consistent gender pattern. In addition to the noun's article, the ending can sometimes help you determine the proper gender; for example, most of the time the nouns ending with "e" are found feminine, but exceptions are there. We can judge through some general pointers, but in the end the advice is always the same: memorize the gender of a word as you learn it.

Masculine Suffixes Sometimes gender may be recognised by the suffix; nouns ending in -age, -ier, -ion, -ment, -oir are masculine.
• Voyage — vwa-zhahzh / व्वाजाझ (Travel / यात्रा)
• Avion — ah-vyohn / आवियों (Aeroplane / हवाई जहाज)
• Bâtiment — baht-ee-mah / बातीमा (Building / इमारत)
• Encrier — ah-kree-ay / ऑँक्रीये (Inkpot / दवात)
• Miroir — meer-wahr / मीरव्हार (Mirror / शीशा)
Feminine Suffixes Nouns that end in -ade, -aison, -tion (-sion), -ance, -erie, -ise, -tude, -te, -ure, -ale, -aix, -oire belong to the feminine group.
• Nation — nah-syohn / नास्यों (Nation / राष्ट्र)
• Substance — sub-stahn-s / सुब्स्तांस (Substance / पदार्थ)
• Maison — may-zõh / मेज़ॉँ (House / घर)
• Bêtise — bay-teez / बेतीज़ (Nonsense / मूर्खता)
• Paix — peh / पे (Peace / शांति)

2. Plural Forms of Nouns (Le Pluriel des Noms)

French: Le Pluriel des Noms / Pronunciation: ल प्लुरिएल दे नॉँ
Plural Rule Category Rule Description & Logic Examples, Pronunciation & Translation
General Rule Most of the nouns are made plural by just adding an "s" to them.
• Un ami → des amis — डे ज़मी (A friend → Some friends / एक मित्र → कई मित्र)
• Un Famille → des Familles — डे फमीय (A family → Families / एक परिवार → परिवार)
• Une ville → des Villes — डे वील (A city → Cities / एक शहर → शहर)
Invariant Endings (-s, -x, -z) Nouns that end in S, X, and Z do not change in the plural form.
• Un autobus → des autobus — डे ओतोबुस (Bus / बसें)
• Un Nez → des nez — डे ने (Nose / नाक)
• Un prix → des prix — डे प्री (Price / कीमत)
Nouns ending in "-al" Nouns that end in "-al" change to "aux" in the plural form.
• Un Journal → des journaux — डे जूर्नो (Newspaper → Newspapers / अखबार)
• Un animal → des animaux — डे आनीमो (Animal → Animals / जानवर)
• Un signal → des Signaux — डे सीग्नो (Signal → Signals / संकेत)
Exceptions (take -s): Un Carnaval → des carnavals (कारनिवल), Un bal → des bals (डांस पार्टी), Un festival → des Festivals (त्योहार).
Nouns ending in "-ail" Nouns that end in "-ail" change to "aux" in the plural form.
• Un travail → des travaux — डे त्रावो (Work → Labors/Works / काम)
Exceptions (take -s): Un détail → des details (विवरण), Un bail → des bailes (लीज).
Nouns ending in "-ou, -eau, -eu, -au" Nouns that end in "ou", "eau", "eu", "au" take X in the plural form.
• Un chou → des Choux — डे शू (Cabbage → Cabbages / गोभी)
• Un Feu → des Feux — डे फू (Fire → Fires / आग)
• Un genou → des genoux — डे झनू (Knee → Knees / घुटना)
Exceptions (take -s): Un Cou → des cous (गर्दन), Un trou → des trous (छेद).
