1. Sound Combinations & Suffixes

French: Les Sons / Pronunciation: ले सॉँ
Topic Details / Definition Rules, Logics & Pronunciation Examples (Correct & Incorrect)
Sound Group 1: é / ez / er / ê Produces an ay / eh vowel sound in French words.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
In French spelling, multiple distinct written forms share this exact sound:
• é always produces a clear "ay" sound (e.g., été).
• -ez at the end of words (like verb conjugations for vous) has a silent z, making it sound identical to é (e.g., parlez).
• -er at the end of words (like 1st group infinitive verbs) has a silent r, making it also sound identical to é (e.g., parler).
• ê (circumflex) shares a close front-vowel quality in many foundational teaching frameworks to help learners recognize that different written letters in French often share the same spoken sound.

French writing is notorious for having multiple ways to spell the exact same spoken sound, which is why teachers group these spelling variations together under one sound rule.

English Comparison: Equivalent to the English 'ay' diphthong (as in DAY).
Hindi Comparison: Equivalent to 'ए' (e.g., 'एक').
Correct Examples:
• été (summer)
  Pronunciation: ay-tay / एते
• parlez (speak)
  Pronunciation: par-lay / पार्ले
• parler (to speak)
  Pronunciation: par-lay / पार्ले
Incorrect Examples:
• ah-tah
  Pronunciation: ah-tah / अता
• par-lez
  Pronunciation: par-lez / पार्लेज़
• par-ler
  Pronunciation: par-ler / पार्लर
Sound Group 2: an / en / ane Produces a nasal 'ahn' sound through the nose.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• an and en produce a nasal 'ahn' sound when followed by a consonant or positioned within a nasal syllable.
• Adding a silent final -e (as in ane) does not alter the nasal vowel quality itself but modifies grammatical gender or syllable division.
• French nasal vowels require expelling air simultaneously through the nose and mouth without pronouncing the trailing hard consonant.

English Comparison: Similar to a nasalized 'on' or 'an' without pronouncing the final hard consonant.
Hindi Comparison: Equivalent to the nasal sound 'ऑँ' or 'हाँ' (Anunasik).
Correct Examples:
• enfant (child)
  Pronunciation: ahn-fahn / ऑँफ ऑँ
• boulanger (baker)
  Pronunciation: bool-ahn-zhay / बुलॉँजे
• dans (in)
  Pronunciation: dahn / ऑँ
Incorrect Examples:
• an-fant
  Pronunciation: an-fant / एनफैंट
• bool-an-gair
  Pronunciation: bool-an-gair / बुलंगेर
Sound Group 3: on Produces a nasal 'ohn' sound through the nose.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• on represents a distinct back nasal vowel sound.
• When a double consonant follows (such as -nn in feminine derivations like bonne), the nasal quality is broken, and a standard oral 'n' sound is pronounced.
• Recognizing this spelling distinction prevents over-nasalizing words where the consonant is doubled.

English Comparison: Nasalized 'on'.
Hindi Comparison: अनुनासिक 'ऑँ'।
Correct Examples:
• bon (good)
  Pronunciation: bohn / बॉँ
• nom (name)
  Pronunciation: nõh / नॉँ
Incorrect Examples:
• bon-n
  Pronunciation: bon-n / बोन
Sound Group 4: in / ain Produces a nasal 'eh' sound through the nose.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• Both in and ain share the identical nasal front-vowel sound.
• The presence of the letter 'i' combined with 'n' or 'in' creates this nasal resonance.
• Adding a trailing vowel or double letter breaks the nasal glide back into a hard consonant sound.

English Comparison: Nasalized 'eh' / 'an'.
Hindi Comparison: अनुनासिक 'ऐं'।
Correct Examples:
• pain (bread)
  Pronunciation: peh / पैं
• fin (end)
  Pronunciation: fẽh / फैं
Incorrect Examples:
• pain-n
  Pronunciation: pain-n / पेन-न
Sound Group 5: un Produces a nasal 'uhn' sound through the nose.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• un represents a rounded nasal vowel.
• While modern Parisian French increasingly merges un with the in/ain sound, formal French maintains a distinct rounded nasal pronunciation.

English Comparison: Nasalized 'uhn'.
Hindi Comparison: अनुनासिक 'अं'।
Correct Examples:
• brun (brown)
  Pronunciation: bruhn / ब्रुँ
• un (one/a)
  Pronunciation: ẽh / अँ
Incorrect Examples:
• un-n
  Pronunciation: un-n / अन-न
Sound Group 6: au / aux / eau Always pronounced as a long 'auh' sound.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• au and eau are multi-vowel letter combinations that always produce a pure long 'oh' sound.
• -aux represents the plural form of words ending in -al (e.g., travail to travaux), where the final 'x' is entirely silent and the sound remains 'oh'.

English Comparison: Matches the English 'o' sound in *BOAT* or *GO*.
Hindi Comparison: Equivalent to 'ओ'.
Correct Examples:
• eau (water)
  Pronunciation: oh / ओ
• bureau (office)
  Pronunciation: byoo-roh / ब्युरो
• chaud (hot)
  Pronunciation: shoh / शो
Incorrect Examples:
• ee-ou
  Pronunciation: ee-ou / ईउ
Sound Group 7: oi Produces a 'wa' sound (as in water).

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• The vowel digraph oi fuses 'o' and 'i' into a rapid 'wa' diphthong.
• It appears frequently across foundational French words, functioning as a single inseparable phonetic unit.

English Comparison: 'wa' glide.
Hindi Comparison: 'वा'।
Correct Examples:
• moi (me)
  Pronunciation: mwa / म्वा
• voilà (there is)
  Pronunciation: vwa-lah / व्वाला
Incorrect Examples:
• oy
  Pronunciation: oy / ओय
Sound Group 8: ment Forms adverbs; pronounced with a nasal 'mãh' sound where 't' is silent.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• -ment is the standard French adverbial suffix equivalent to English '-ly'.
• Following French suffix rules, the final 't' is silent, and the preceding 'en' creates a nasalized 'mãh' sound.

English Comparison: Adverb suffix '-ly' with silent 't'.
Hindi Comparison: 'माँ'।
Correct Examples:
• lentement (slowly)
  Pronunciation: lahnt-mãh / लांमाँ
• vraiment (truly/really)
  Pronunciation: vray-mah / व्रय-मा
Incorrect Examples:
• lentement-t
  Pronunciation: lahnt-ment / लांमेंट
Sound Group 9: tion Pronounced with a syou / syohn sound.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• -tion is pronounced with a 'syohn' sound rather than the English '-shun'.
• The letter 't' retains a soft 's' friction before the 'ion' vowel glide.

English Comparison: French '-syohn' instead of English '-shun'.
Hindi Comparison: 'स्यों'।
Correct Examples:
• action (action)
  Pronunciation: ak-syohn / आकस्यों
• attention (attention)
  Pronunciation: ah-tahn-syohn / ऑँतस्यों
Incorrect Examples:
• ti-on
  Pronunciation: tik-shun / टिक्शन
Sound Group 10: ill / gn Produces soft 'i / iy' for 'ill' and 'ny' for 'gn'.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• ill after vowels creates a soft glide (e.g., fille).
• gn produces a palatal nasal sound similar to 'ny' in canyon (e.g., gagne).

English Comparison: 'y' in yes or 'ny' in canyon.
Hindi Comparison: 'य' या 'न्य'।
Correct Examples:
• fille (daughter/girl)
  Pronunciation: fee / फ़ी
• gagne (wins/earns)
  Pronunciation: gany / गान्य
Incorrect Examples:
• fill-e
  Pronunciation: fill / फिल्

2. Silent Rules & Sounded Consonants

French: Les Règles Muettes & Consonnes / Pronunciation: ले रेग्ल म्यूएत
Topic Details / Definition Rules, Logics & Pronunciation Examples (Correct & Incorrect)
The Letter 'H' The letter 'H' is always completely silent in French.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• French divides 'h' into two categories: h muet (mute h, which permits elision like l'homme and liaison) and h aspiré (aspirated h, which blocks elision like le héros).
• In both cases, the letter 'h' itself is never pronounced as an English aspirate sound.

English Comparison: Unaspirated unlike English *house*.
Hindi Comparison: मून वर्ण (कोई 'ह' नहीं)।
Correct Examples:
• homme (man)
  Pronunciation: ohm / ओम
• hôtel (hotel)
  Pronunciation: oh-tel / ओतेल
Incorrect Examples:
• hahm
  Pronunciation: hahm / हाम
Final Silent Consonants Final consonants like -s, -t, -x, -z, -d, -p are generally silent at the end of words.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• Unless modified by liaison or part of the CaReFuL QD rule, ending consonants are dropped in standard pronunciation.

English Comparison: Dropping final hard sounds.
Hindi Comparison: अंतिम व्यंजन मूक।
Correct Examples:
• petit (small/little)
  Pronunciation: puh-tee / पुती
Incorrect Examples:
• puh-teet
  Pronunciation: puh-teet / पुतीत
Sounded Consonants (CaReFuL QD) Letters C, R, F, L, Q, D are sounded at the end of words.

Detailed Spelling Rules & Orthography Logic:
• Memorized via the mnemonic acronym CaReFuL QD (C, R, F, L, Q, D).
• These specific final consonants break the general rule of silent endings and must be articulated fully.

English Comparison: Pronouncing final hard consonants.
Hindi Comparison: अंत में पूरे व्यंजन का उच्चारण।
Correct Examples:
• sac (bag) — Pronunciation: sak / सैक
• mer (sea) — Pronunciation: mair / मेर
Incorrect Examples:
• sa (silent c)

4. French Accents

French: Les Accents / Pronunciation: ले आक्सॉँ
Topic Details / Definition Rules, Logics & Pronunciation Examples (Correct & Incorrect)
L'Accent Aigu ( ´ ) — Acute Accent Accent Name Pronunciation:
English: lahn-sahn eh-goo
Hindi: लां-सॉं ए-गू.

Rule: Used ONLY on 'e' for a closed 'ay' sound (like English day). Never used before double consonants or final silent consonants.

English Comparison: Equivalent to English long 'a'.
Hindi Comparison: Equivalent to vowel sign 'े'.
Correct Examples:
• école (school) — ay-kohl / एकोल
• été (summer) — ay-tay / एते
• café (coffee) — ka-fay / कैफे
Incorrect Examples:
• ah-kohl (अकोल)
L'Accent Grave ( ` ) — Grave Accent Accent Name Pronunciation:
English: lahn-sahn grahv
Hindi: लां-सॉं ग्राव.

Rule: Used on 'e' (open 'eh' sound like bed), and on 'a' or 'u' to distinguish homophones (e.g., à = to vs. a = has).

English Comparison: Equivalent to open 'e' in bed.
Hindi Comparison: Equivalent to vowel sign 'ै'.
Correct Examples:
• frère (brother) — frair / फ्रेर
• mère (mother) — mair / मेर
• où (where) — oo / ऊ
Incorrect Examples:
• freer (फ्रीर)
L'Accent Circonflexe ( ^ ) — Circumflex Accent Name Pronunciation:
English: lahn-sahn seer-kohn-fleks
Hindi: लां-सॉं सीरकॉन्फ्लेक्स.

Rule: Used on all vowels. Often marks historical loss of 's' from Old French (feste → fête).

English Comparison: Lengthens vowel sound.
Hindi Comparison: Long vowel marker (दीर्घ स्वर).
Correct Examples:
• être (to be) — et-tr / एत्र
• fête (party) — fet / फेत
• tête (head) — tet / तेत
Incorrect Examples:
• ee-tr (ईत्र)
La Cédille ( ç ) — Cedilla Accent Name Pronunciation:
English: lah say-dee-yuh
Hindi: ला सेदीयु.

Rule: Transforms hard 'c' into a soft 's' sound before 'a', 'o', and 'u'.

English Comparison: Like 's' in snake.
Hindi Comparison: Like 'स'.
Correct Examples:

English: luh tray-mah

5. Definite Articles

French: Les Articles Définis / Pronunciation: ले आर्तिक्ल देफिनी
Topic Details / Definition Rules, Logics & Pronunciation Examples (Correct & Incorrect)
Le (Masculine Singular) Used before masculine singular nouns starting with a consonant. (English: *the*)
Correct: • le stylo (pen) — luh stee-loh / ल स्टीलो
Incorrect: • le table
La (Feminine Singular) Used before feminine singular nouns starting with a consonant. (English: *the*)
Correct: • la table (table) — lah tah-bl / ला ताब्ल
Incorrect: • la stylo
L' (Vowels & Silent H) Used before any singular noun starting with a vowel (a, e, i, o, u) or silent 'h' (Elision).
Correct: • l'école (school) — lay-kohl / लेकोल
Incorrect: • le école
Les (Plural Nouns) Used before plural nouns regardless of gender.
Correct: • les stylos (pens) — lay stee-loh / ले स्टीलो
Incorrect: • le stylos
