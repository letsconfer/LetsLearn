3. Complex Base-20 Numbers (81 to 100)

French: Les Nombres de 81 à 100 / Pronunciation: ले नॉँब्र द कात्र-वैं-अँ आ सॉँ
Vigesimal Formula (Base-20 Structure):
• 80 = quatre-vingts (4 × 20). Note the plural "s" on vingts when standalone.
• 81-89: Drop the "s" from vingt and add unit numbers (e.g., 81 = quatre-vingt-un [four-twenty-one]). Do NOT use et for 81.
• 90-99: Combine quatre-vingt with 10-19 (e.g., 90 = quatre-vingt-dix [four-twenty-ten], 91 = quatre-vingt-onze [four-twenty-eleven]).

4. Hundreds Structure (200 to 900)

French: Les Centaines / Pronunciation: ले सॉँतेन
Plural Rule for Cent:
• Multiple exact hundreds take a plural "s" (e.g., 200 = deux cents, 300 = trois cents, 400 = quatre cents).
• If another number follows, drop the "s" from cent (e.g., 202 = deux cent deux, 301 = trois cent un).

5. Thousands & Higher Scale Numbers

French: Les Milliers et Plus / Pronunciation: ले मीलीये ए प्ल्यु
Grammar Rule for Mille (1,000):
• Mille is completely invariant: It NEVER takes an "s" in the plural (e.g., 2,000 = deux mille, NEVER deux milles).
• Million & Milliard are nouns: They DO take an "s" in the plural (e.g., 2,000,000 = deux millions).
