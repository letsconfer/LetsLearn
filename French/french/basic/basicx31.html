French Spelling Cheat Codes, Cognates & False Friends Reference
True cognates (vrais amis) are words shared between English and French that have the exact same meaning and identical or near-identical spellings. Because English inherited nearly 30% to 40% of its vocabulary from French after the Norman Conquest of 1066, you can use highly predictable "cheat codes" to instantly unlock thousands of words.

The rules, suffixes, and patterns where words remain identical or require minimal, programmatic adjustments to fit both languages are outlined below.

2. Suffix Conversion Cheat Codes (Near-Identical)

French: Les Conversions de Suffixes / Pronunciation: ले कॉनवेरस्यों द सुफीक्स
If a word doesn't match perfectly, it usually follows a strict spelling mutation rule. You can swap the endings to effortlessly glide between English and French:
ENGLISH ENDING FRENCH SWAP RULE & LOGIC EXAMPLES (ENGLISH → FRENCH)
-ty → -té Abstract noun conversion mapping English '-ty' to French '-té' with acute accent.
• Activity → Activité
• Beauty → Beauté
• Capacity → Capacité
• Purity → Pureté
-ory / -ary → -oire / -aire Descriptive and institutional suffix mapping.
• Adversary → Adversaire
• Military → Militaire
• Victory → Victoire
• Memory → Mémoire
-ous → -eux Adjectival ending conversion.
• Dangerous → Dangereux
• Generous → Généreux
• Furious → Furieux
-ic / -ical → -ique Scientific and logical descriptor mapping.
• Logic / Logical → Logique
• Music → Musique
• Physical → Physique
-ism & -ist → -isme & -iste Ideologies and professions.
• Artist → Artiste
• Tourist → Touriste
• Racism → Racisme
-ly → -ment Standard adverbial suffix conversion.
• Rapidly → Rapidement
• General → Généralement

4. The History "Cheat Codes" (Hidden S & É Mapping)

French: Les Codes Historiques / Pronunciation: ले कोद ओस्तोरिक
If you see a French word with a circumflex accent (ˆ) over a vowel, it almost always means an "S" used to sit right after that vowel in Old French. English borrowed the words before the French dropped the "S"!

Similarly, a French word beginning with é- often maps to an English word starting with s-.
HISTORICAL PATTERN ETYMOLOGICAL LOGIC FRENCH → ENGLISH EXAMPLES
Circumflex (Â, Ê, Î, Ô, Û) Represents historical loss of an 's' after the vowel.
• Hôpital → Hospital
• Forêt → Forest
• Ancêtre → Ancestor
• Côte → Coast
• Pâte → Paste
É- Prefix Mapping French words starting with é- often correspond to English words starting with s-.
• Étrange → Strange
• Étudier → Study
• Éponge → Sponge

⚠️ Critical Warning: The "False Friends" (Faux Amis)

French: Les Faux Amis / Pronunciation: ले फ़ो ज़ामी
While thousands of words line up beautifully, a few dangerous "trap" words look identical but mean completely different things. Always remember these major exceptions:
FRENCH WORD TRUE FRENCH MEANING & LOGIC FALSE ENGLISH FRIEND (TRAP)
Actuel / Actuellement Current / Currently / Right now. (To say "actual", use réel). ❌ NOT "Actual"
Blesser To injure or hurt. (To bless is bénir). ❌ NOT "To Bless"
Librairie Bookstore. (A public library is la bibliothèque). ❌ NOT "Library"
Attendre To wait for. (To attend an event is assister à). ❌ NOT "To Attend"
Préservatif Condom. (A food preservative is un conservateur). ❌ NOT "Preservative"
