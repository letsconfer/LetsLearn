Les Prépositions (Prepositions)

Comprehensive Guide to French Prepositions, Countries, Cities, Time & Location

Introduction to Prepositions

French: Les Prépositions / Pronunciation: ले प्रेपोझ़िसियों

What are prepositions? Prepositions are small linking words that connect elements of a sentence together and indicate relationships of place, direction, time, or origin between words. Prepositions cannot always be translated literally word-for-word; they must be learned in context with their specific usage rules.

1. Prepositions with Countries, Continents, Provinces & Cities

French: Au / En / À & Du / De / Des + Noms de Pays / Pronunciation: ओ / आँ / आ & द्यू / द / दे + नों द पेई
Topic Details / Definition Rules, Logics & Pronunciation Examples (Correct & Incorrect)
Going to / Being in (Aller / Être / Habiter)

• en
• au
• aux
• à		 How to express going to or being in a geographical location:

1. en: Always use before
• feminine countries, continents, provinces,
• and masculine countries starting with a vowel.

Important Note:
• en is never followed by articles like le, du, des.
• Also used for years (en 1983), months (en janvier), seasons (en hiver), and mode of transport (en train).

2. au: Use it before masculine singular countries starting with a consonant.

3. aux: Use before plural countries
(e.g., aux États-Unis, aux Antilles, aux Philippines).

4. à: Use before all cities and towns
(e.g., à Paris, à Mumbai, à Delhi).
Correct Examples:
• Je vais en Suisse. (I am going to Switzerland) — /झ़ वे आं स्वीस/
• Il travaille en Iran. (He works in Iran) — /इल त्रावाय आं ईरां/
• Elle va au Japon. (She goes to Japan) — /एल वा ओ झ़ापों/
• Ils vont aux États-Unis. (They go to the USA) — /इल वों ओज़ एताज़-यूनी/
• Je vais à Paris. (I am going to Paris) — /झ़ वे आ पारी/
Incorrect Examples:
• Je vais au Suisse. (Wrong gender article)
• Il va en Japon. (Wrong preposition for masculine country)
Coming from / Belonging to (Venir / Être de)

• de / d’
• du
• des		 How to express coming from or belonging to a place:

1. de: Used before feminine countries, continents, provinces starting with a consonant (e.g., de Suisse, de France). Also used for cities (e.g., de Paris).

2. d’ / de: Used before any country or city starting with a vowel (e.g., d’Amérique, d’Inde, d’Iran, d’Afghanistan).

3. du: Used before masculine singular countries starting with a consonant (e.g., du Japon, du Danemark).

4. des: Used before plural countries (e.g., des États-Unis, des Antilles, des Émirats).
Correct Examples:
• Je viens de Suisse. (I come from Switzerland) — /झ़ व्यें द स्वीस/
• Il vient d’Inde. (He comes from India) — /इल व्यें दैंद/
• Elle vient du Japon. (She comes from Japan) — /एल व्यें द्यू झ़ापों/
• Je viens des États-Unis. (I come from the USA) — /झ़ व्यें देज़ एताज़-यूनी/
Incorrect Examples:
• Je viens de Japon. (Missing masculine contraction du)
Important Exceptions & Plurals • Masculine countries ending in -e: Most countries ending in -e are feminine, but these 4 are masculine and take au / du: le Mexique, le Mozambique, le Zaïre, le Zimbabwe.

• Plural country names: Les Philippines, Les Antilles, Les États-Unis, Les Pays-Bas (take aux / des).
Correct Examples:
• Mes parents sont au Mexique. (My parents are in Mexico)
• Il vient du Mexique. (He comes from Mexico)

2. Prepositions of Place: chez, dans, sur, sous

French: Les Prépositions de lieu (2): chez, dans, sur, sous / Pronunciation: ले प्रेपोझ़िसियों द ल्यू: शे, दां, स्यूर, सू
French English Meaning Usage & Examples
chez at / to (someone's place)
i.e Used before a person's name
or a profession/shop title.		 • Je vais chez Christophe. (I am going to Christophe's house)
• Il va chez le coiffeur. / chez le fleuriste. (He is going to the hairdresser's / florist's)
dans in / inside / into
Used for enclosed physical spaces, rooms, buildings,
cities, countries (with articles), and specific containers or vehicles.
 • J’habite dans la ville. (I live in the city)
• Je voyage dans le train. (I am traveling inside the train — contrasted with en train by train)
sur on / over
Used to indicate directly
on or over a surface.
 • Le livre est sur la table. (The book is on the table)
• Sur la tête. (On the head)
sous under
Used to indicate directly under something.
 • Le chat est sous la table. (The cat is under the table)
